## The brief, to myself

The previous version had a Laravel backend, a Postgres database, a Next.js
frontend, and a React Native companion app, all to publish a post every couple
of weeks. This time the rule was: stop overbuilding.

## What it is

One static Astro site. Posts and projects live as MDX files, so publishing is
a commit and a push. A dithered shader sits behind the home wordmark, and
apart from the schematics below everything else is type and hairlines. The
contact form posts to a tiny
Cloudflare Worker that calls Resend server-side, which keeps the site fully
static with no backend to babysit. It deploys to my own VPS through Coolify.

## The schematics

Each of the six things on the [skills page](/skills/) has a drawing: 1.5px
lines over a dot grid, which draws itself and then does its job on a loop. On a
phone it is an SVG moved by GSAP. On a desktop the same drawing is real
geometry in three.js, framed through a long lens so it reads as flat until you
hover, when the camera moves in and the perspective appears.

The SVG is the default and the 3D is the enhancement, loaded only where there
is a pointer to hover with, WebGL to draw with, and no request for reduced
motion. Nothing on the page depends on it arriving.

## What I kept

The pink accent, the monospace details, and the idea that a personal site can
be a little weird without being a maintenance burden.
